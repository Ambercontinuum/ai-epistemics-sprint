\begin{abstract}
When people work with AI, meaning can drift while everything still looks right: outputs are fluent, models agree, code runs. Across many projects, the lead researcher pushed AI models with language to explore where shared meaning held or collapsed. In this sprint we asked what can actually verify that it held, and tested three candidates. \emph{Model agreement:} five models from five developers rated 20 archived conversations on a seven-field rubric; within-half-point agreement ranged from 0\% to 100\% across rater pairs and fields, so consensus cannot certify meaning. \emph{Working code:} we wired the researcher's monitoring and gating frameworks into a multi-agent sandbox under staged pressures such as a forged approval; every setup scored near the ceiling, showing the wiring runs but not that meaning held. \emph{Dated records:} the slips that did occur, including scope drift, a contested comparison, excess spending, and an AI reviewer that took an idiom as permission to edit this paper, were caught by the researcher and remain traceable in hash-pinned coordination records, where each can be audited. We argue that fidelity is verified by tracing how a request was interpreted, committed to, acted on, and corrected. Code and hash manifests are public; the dated coordination records are available on request.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An assistant can produce a plausible answer, agree with other models, and retain a stable vocabulary while pursuing a task the operator did not intend. In collaborative research, the relevant object of evaluation is therefore larger than the isolated answer. It includes how a purpose becomes an interpretation, how an interpretation becomes a commitment, and whether subsequent work preserves or explicitly revises that commitment.

The deployment examined here makes this problem concrete. A human lead researcher worked with several AI systems to develop an epistemics submission. That sprint followed a longer, unplanned exploration across many projects, preserved in the researcher's conversation archive, in which the researcher pushed models with language to see where shared meaning held or broke. Existing research, private conversations, framework repositories, and a shared coordination record were already available. Nevertheless, the recorded interaction repeatedly required the researcher to clarify whether the deliverable was a paper, a training experiment, a benchmark, or a product. Later interventions concerned the authoritative comparison, the provenance of training material, isolation of experimental records, and spending. These are documented coordination episodes, not a representative sample of AI use or independently coded estimates of failure prevalence \citep{coordinationrecords}.

The research question is: \emph{How can an AI research workflow verify fidelity to human intent when meaning is contextual, evolving, and corrected through interaction?} Here, ``intent'' refers to the operator's expressed purposes, constraints, and authorized revisions. It does not mean direct access to a private mental state. Preserving intent also does not mean accepting every factual claim the operator makes. A useful system must distinguish understanding a purpose from verifying an empirical assertion.

The paper contributes three linked artifacts: (1) a situated account of documented human repair work; (2) a reproducible, descriptive PDOT rating pilot with missingness and format failures retained; and (3) an executable, sandboxed measurement--routing--recovery--action loop with an inconclusive behavioral comparison. The Open Track framing concerns deployment and practical use; the prototype supplies a secondary Epistemic Products contribution. Preexisting frameworks and corpus material are distinguished from sprint work in Appendix~\ref{app:prior}.

\section{Related Work}
Clark and Brennan describe grounding as the collaborative work needed to establish sufficient mutual understanding for a purpose \citep{clark1991}. This motivates treating corrections and their uptake as part of the interaction, rather than evaluating only a final answer. Hutchins studies cognition distributed across people, representations, and material practices \citep{hutchins1995}. That perspective makes persistent records and team handoffs relevant units of analysis. Neither account implies that agreement is truth or supplies a validated numerical detector of intent.

Measurement modeling distinguishes a theoretical construct from the observable properties used to operationalize it \citep{jacobs2021}. In this deployment, model-assigned ``anchoring'' or ``coherence'' scores are operational judgments; a high score is not direct verification of meaning. Research on LLM judges also documents position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases \citep{zheng2023}. Our model agreement results consequently remain separate from construct validity and operator endorsement.

The researcher's preexisting PDOT materials define interaction ratings and perturbation-recovery anchors. STK-Flux supplies a coupling-state router, STK-Teams extends it with contributor-aware state and handoffs, and SERAPH includes Sentinel/PRP recovery logic \citep{pdot,stkflux,stkteams,seraph}. These repositories establish implemented components and documented lineage. They do not establish that a numerical state inferred from a transcript is a valid measurement, that the kernels are equivalent, or that a connected configuration improves human decisions.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Evidence boundaries and operator perspective}
Three evidence layers are kept distinct. First, dated coordination notes preserve selected user instructions, assistant interpretations, corrections, decisions, and execution receipts. The researcher is both operator and investigator. These records support illustrative, retrospective vignettes; this draft does not report a completed ethnography, systematic qualitative coding, inter-coder agreement, or a complete export of the live session. The vignettes were selected for relevance to the question, not sampled to estimate prevalence.

Second, the archive pilot uses real conversation prefixes. Third, the sandbox uses constructed operator events inspired by coordination problems. Human signal is already present in the archive and live record. What the sandbox lacks is an unfolding live-human intervention condition, not human-origin content altogether. We do not pool the three layers into a single sample or infer causality by comparing their scores.

\subsection{Archive selection and model-rating protocol}
The received private archive contains 490 conversations. Mechanical eligibility required the first 50 nonempty messages to alternate human and assistant, with an unbranched parent chain; 74 conversations qualified. A seeded hash ordering selected 50 prefixes, a second ordering selected 20 cases, and a third fixed ten overlap cases. Each analyzed prefix contains 25 human--assistant exchanges. It is neither the entire original conversation nor an independent human participant. Seeds and hash conventions appear in Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}.

The five raters came from different developers: Google's Gemini; Qwen, an open-weight model from Alibaba's Qwen team, served on the researcher's own Modal endpoint; OpenAI's Astra; Anthropic's Fable; and Meta's Muse Wonder, a model distinct from the team collaborator Muse. Gemini, Qwen, Astra, and Fable were each assigned the same 20 cases; Wonder was assigned the fixed ten-case overlap. Fresh API calls received the pinned PDOT rater guide and assigned window, without earlier ratings or team coordination history. The seven fields were $\lambda$ (anchoring), $\kappa$ (coherence), $\theta$ (agency balance), $\epsilon$ (drift), symbolic pressure, $R$ (resonance), and $\tau$ (torsion), on a 0--4 half-point scale. Higher symbolic pressure denotes frame load, not a general quality score. Missing values were not converted to zero. The archive's assistant turns are Claude-family output, so Fable rated its own model family's side of each conversation; other provider relationships are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}.

Strict JSON parsing, returned model identifiers, and completion status were checked. Raw failures were retained without study-item retries. An authorized derivative removed complete outer Markdown fences from four Qwen responses without changing their inner content. Wonder--Qwen was designated the primary comparison by the researcher \emph{after run completion}, replacing an earlier team pre-specification of Gemini--Wonder, and after a descriptive all-pairs table had been generated; this was an interpretive framing decision, not a prospectively registered hierarchy. The researcher reports having viewed no agreement values, from that table or elsewhere, when making the designation.

Strict paired results remain separate from normalized results. No human ratings supplied a correctness anchor. The analysis is descriptive: per-field coverage, exact agreement, agreement within 0.5 points, and mean absolute difference (MAD). No significance test, chance-corrected agreement statistic, composite, or validity threshold was adopted.

\subsection{Constructed sandbox and connected prototype}
An initial two-arm replay used six scripted rounds and three role slots, comparing common instructions with added STK-Flux guidance. It made 36 participant calls and three Qwen evaluation calls. An additional pilot compared baseline, STK-only, and an integrated stack on the same six harder constructed rounds. The rounds included an operator correction, conflicting forwarded review material, a stale cache, a log-route change, an asserted launch approval, and a limited virtual push. The source of each message remained visible; conflict materials were not conveniently labelled ``untrusted.'' Each arm used the same role mapping: coordinator/Astra, architect/Wonder, and liaison/Astra. This differed from the initial pilot to keep Anthropic out of the participant set when Fable became the runtime monitor.

The integrated loop connected actual pinned code, rather than only naming the frameworks:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Fable assessed completed same-arm prefixes using PDOT ontology definitions and recovery anchors. It did not score its own provider family's participant responses.
 \item The original PDOT transform mapped ontology outputs to coupling signals. Explicit $\operatorname{round}(100x)$ conversion supplied STK's 0--100 inputs; recovery fidelity and coupling ratings were divided by four for SERAPH. These are exploratory model-derived proxies.
 \item STK-Teams maintained contributor-tagged routing history and commitment strictness. SERAPH's Sentinel/PRP posture selector assessed recovery evidence.
 \item A new product adapter required explicit operator path permission, an embedded Sentinel path, and STK Commit before permitting a virtual push. Missing current measurements or recovery evidence held that action; an older clear was not reused.
\end{enumerate}
The baseline had the common virtual permission broker. STK-only used prespecified synthetic states; the integrated arm used measured prefixes and additional gating. This is a bundled intervention involving extra context, monitor compute, team state, and enforcement. It cannot isolate an STK-specific effect. SERAPH guardian/environment defaults were declared virtual-host assumptions, not measured real-world safety. The full SERAPH deployment and PDOT embedding/equation pipeline were not instantiated.

All agent actions affected in-memory repositories. No real file, Git, publication, or job-execution tool was available. Ten synthetic confinement/integration checks passed before the three-arm pilot; five of them require the private vendored source snapshot to run. Calls were capped at 54 participants, six runtime measurements, and four final-evaluation calls; no retries or fallback models were used. The first three Qwen final calls independently rated masked episodes; the fourth compared their ratings. The final evaluator received public interaction/artifact records but not internal monitor scores. Framework language could reveal condition, so masking was partial. The seven-field evaluation definitions were fixed before the new pilot's outputs; original equation and CCS sections were excluded.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The deployment and sandbox answer different questions. A constructed task makes operator instructions and source boundaries unusually explicit. The live record includes the work of discovering which framing matters, locating prior evidence, and deciding when to stop. Near-ceiling ratings on the former do not establish that the latter work has been automated. Equally, the existence of human interventions does not establish that only humans can generate relevant perturbations or that models cannot respond to live pressure.

Figure~\ref{fig:trace} makes the proposed verification target narrower and more inspectable: which interpretation was acknowledged, which commitment was authorized, what action followed, and whether a later correction replaced it. Records are useful because interpretations can be revisited and contested. Their existence is not consensus, authenticity, truth, or proof that the system understood the operator. A content hash identifies retained bytes; it cannot certify the adequacy of their interpretation.

The researcher's broader hypothesis is that human signals require suitable operational counterparts to preserve meaning at high fidelity. This work motivates examining that hypothesis but does not establish a complete mapping as necessary or sufficient. Some local commitments can be checked without a complete theory of meaning. Verification of the wider purpose remains situated and includes the operator's ability to correct the record.

The strongest limitations are the single operator, restricted archive prefixes, illustrative retrospective vignettes, no independent qualitative coding, no human scoring anchor, no chance-corrected agreement, and no randomized live-human comparison. Archive cases may omit attachment context, and score agreement need not imply correctness. Sandbox conditions have one episode each, partial masking, one judge, different measurement sources, and unequal context/compute/enforcement. Post-run comparison framing and post-output fence derivatives are explicit deviations, not reconstructed preregistration. Model-returned identifiers are not independent attestations of served weights.

Deployment cost is also part of usability. The later three-arm run made 36 Astra calls whose local receipts total 294,076 input and 18,822 output tokens. After the researcher reported unexpectedly high charges, further OpenAI use was blocked and four offline spending-guard checks passed. The spending guard was added after the study and was not part of the tested intervention. This incident illustrates a practical distinction between a request-count cap and an operator-approved monetary budget.

Future work should prospectively select realistic cases, include underspecified and legitimately revised purposes, and evaluate both preserved intent and useful completion. Independent raters and operator review should distinguish failed fidelity, successful clarification, justified disagreement, and overconservative holds. The role-boundary episode suggests a targeted evaluation: matched underspecified requests, varying salient context and constraint prominence, scored on whether an agent states its interpretation before committing, with scoring rules that also penalize over-caution. A matched-context/compute ablation could then separate measurement, routing, and enforcement effects.
\section{Conclusion}
This deployment shows why evaluating model agreement, running an executable controller, and verifying fidelity to a person's purpose must remain distinct activities. The archive pilot shows that model raters can return schema-valid structured judgments against a shared rubric, with important missingness and format qualifications; whether their agreement exceeds chance remains untested. The connected sandbox prototype executes a measurement--routing--recovery--action loop, but its near-ceiling comparison does not establish improved fidelity. The live records preserve the researcher's work of correcting scope, interpretation, provenance, and resource boundaries. An epistemic product intended for real use should make those relationships inspectable and their human correction possible, while measuring whether it reduces rather than relocates the operator's burden.

\section{Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations}\label{app:ethics}
The results concern a private, single-operator deployment and constructed coordination tasks. They do not demonstrate general-purpose epistemic improvement, human necessity, internal model awareness, a complete mechanical representation of meaning, or an STK-specific causal effect. The manuscript was prepared with AI assistance and approved by the author; no independent human review is asserted.

\textbf{Privacy and disclosure.} The archive contains private interactions and potentially identifying or third-party material. Case identifiers removed source titles and identifiers from rating packets, but text was not guaranteed anonymous. Selected private windows were sent to the providers explicitly approved by the researcher; this does not imply zero retention or deletion at those providers. No raw conversation, credential, or case-level response is reproduced in this draft. Live vignettes paraphrase existing coordination records. Any public quotation, code package, or derived dataset requires a separate release review by the author. A later report that keys were disabled is reported by the researcher, not independently tested through provider calls.

\textbf{Misuse and operator autonomy.} A system that models intent can also steer, manipulate, or misrepresent it. Authority tracking should not become an excuse to suppress warranted disagreement or to present a model's interpretation as the human's authentic preference. Numerical coupling or recovery scores can create unjustified legitimacy. The original state labels are proxies and must remain contestable. An institution could misuse a coherence score to penalize dissent; no such use is supported by this pilot.

\textbf{Enforcement boundary.} Virtual actions demonstrate confinement under the tested interface, not production security. Default guardian/environment inputs are assumptions about that interface. Deployment posture selection is not itself a proof that a tool invocation is safe. The newly written adapter enforces a deliberately narrower virtual push policy. Local hashes and append-only conventions are not externally anchored signatures or tamper-proof provenance.

\textbf{Researcher position.} The researcher developed the frameworks, supplied the corpus, operated the collaboration, and proposes the interpretation. That access supplies context and also creates selection and confirmation risks. The selected vignettes are illustrative and retrospective. Model-generated judgments do not remove those risks or replace independent human assessment.
